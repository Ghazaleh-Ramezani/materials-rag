Reduced Graphene Oxide Strain Sensors with Tunable Gauge Factor

Abstract
Reduced graphene oxide (rGO) has emerged as a versatile material for flexible
strain sensors owing to its high electrical conductivity and mechanical
flexibility. In this work we describe the fabrication of rGO-based piezoresistive
sensors and the relationship between reduction degree and sensing performance.

Introduction
Graphene oxide (GO) is an electrically insulating precursor that can be reduced
chemically or thermally to restore a conductive sp2 carbon network. The degree of
reduction controls the carrier concentration and therefore the baseline resistance
of the film. Sensors built from highly reduced rGO show lower baseline resistance
but a smaller relative resistance change under strain.

Sensing Mechanism
Under tensile strain, microcracks form between overlapping rGO flakes, increasing
the tunneling distance and raising resistance. The gauge factor, defined as the
relative change in resistance divided by the applied strain, increases with the
density of flake-flake junctions. We observed gauge factors ranging from 12 at low
reduction to over 60 for partially reduced films with abundant junctions.

Results
Partially reduced rGO films achieved a gauge factor of 64 at 2 percent strain while
retaining stable response over 5000 bending cycles. Increasing reduction temperature
from 200 to 400 degrees Celsius lowered baseline resistance from 8 kilo-ohm to
0.9 kilo-ohm but reduced the gauge factor due to fewer high-resistance junctions.

Conclusion
The reduction degree of rGO is the primary lever for tuning the trade-off between
baseline conductivity and strain sensitivity. Partial reduction offers the best
compromise for wearable strain-sensing applications.

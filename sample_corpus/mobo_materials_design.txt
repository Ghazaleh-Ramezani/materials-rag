Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization for Materials Design

Abstract
Designing materials often requires balancing competing objectives such as strength
and ductility, or conductivity and transparency. We apply multi-objective Bayesian
optimization to navigate these trade-offs efficiently, identifying Pareto-optimal
compositions with a limited experimental budget.

Background
Bayesian optimization builds a surrogate model, typically a Gaussian process, over
an expensive black-box objective and uses an acquisition function to select the next
experiment. In the multi-objective setting the goal is to approximate the Pareto
front rather than a single optimum.

Acquisition Function
We use the expected hypervolume improvement acquisition function, which scores
candidate points by how much they are expected to expand the dominated hypervolume.
The log variant qLogEHVI improves numerical stability and supports batch proposals,
allowing several experiments to be suggested per iteration.

Case Study
Applied to a composite formulation problem with two objectives, the method located
a Pareto front covering high-stiffness and high-toughness regions within 40
evaluations, compared with over 150 evaluations for random search. The surrogate
uncertainty guided sampling toward poorly explored composition regions.

Conclusion
Multi-objective Bayesian optimization with hypervolume-based acquisition is a
sample-efficient route to mapping property trade-offs in materials design, reducing
the number of costly experiments required.

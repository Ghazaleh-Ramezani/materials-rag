Nanocellulose-Graphene Composite Films for Humidity Sensing

Abstract
Cellulose nanofibrils (CNF) provide a hydrophilic, mechanically robust matrix that
can host conductive graphene fillers. We report flexible humidity sensors made from
CNF films loaded with graphene oxide, where water uptake modulates ionic and
electronic transport.

Matrix and Filler
CNF is extracted from wood pulp by mechanical fibrillation and forms transparent,
foldable films through vacuum filtration. Graphene oxide disperses well in the polar
CNF matrix because of abundant oxygen-containing groups on both components, yielding
uniform composites without surfactants.

Humidity Response
At low relative humidity the composite conducts mainly through percolating graphene
oxide pathways. As relative humidity rises above 60 percent, absorbed water
introduces proton hopping that sharply increases conductivity. The sensor showed a
response time of about 8 seconds and recovery time of 15 seconds across a 20 to 90
percent relative humidity range.

Mechanical Properties
Adding 5 weight percent graphene oxide increased the tensile strength of the CNF
film from 90 to 130 megapascal while keeping elongation at break above 4 percent.
Higher loadings beyond 10 weight percent embrittled the film and reduced humidity
sensitivity due to filler aggregation.

Conclusion
CNF-graphene oxide composites combine mechanical robustness with strong humidity
response, making them attractive for sustainable, biodegradable sensing substrates.

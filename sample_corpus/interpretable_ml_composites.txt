Interpretable Machine Learning for Predicting Composite Properties

Abstract
Machine learning models can predict the mechanical and electrical properties of
composites from processing and composition descriptors, but black-box predictions
hinder scientific trust. We use SHAP-based interpretability to explain a gradient
boosting model trained on a composite property dataset.

Dataset and Model
The dataset contains composition fractions, filler aspect ratio, and processing
temperature as input features, with electrical conductivity as the target. A
gradient boosting regressor achieved a coefficient of determination of 0.91 on a
held-out test set.

Feature Attribution
SHAP values decompose each prediction into additive feature contributions. Filler
loading and aspect ratio were the dominant positive contributors to conductivity,
while excessive processing temperature contributed negatively, consistent with known
filler degradation at high temperature.

Physical Consistency
The interpretability analysis recovered established physical relationships: a
percolation-like threshold in filler loading and a monotonic benefit from higher
aspect ratio. This agreement increases confidence that the model captures genuine
structure-property relationships rather than spurious correlations.

Conclusion
Pairing accurate predictive models with SHAP attribution provides both predictive
power and physical insight, supporting interpretable, data-driven materials design.
